# **1. The Rise of Nationalism in Europe**

###  **French Revolution & Nationalism**

* Introduced **modern nationalism**.
* Spread ideas of **liberty**, **equality before law**, **nation of citizens**.
* New symbols of nation  national flag, national anthem.
* Administrative reforms: **uniform laws**, standard weights & measures.

---

###  **Napoleons Role**

Positive:

* Civil Code of 1804 (Napoleonic Code)  equality, property protection, abolition of feudal privileges.
  Negative:
* Expansion through war  many saw France as an occupying force.

---

###  **Liberalism**

* Meant **freedom for individuals** and **equality before law**.
* In economics  **free markets**, removal of internal tariffs, single currency.

---

###  **Romanticism**

* Emphasised **culture**, **language**, **folk tales**, **songs** as unity tools.
* Example: Johann Gottfried Herder  folk culture = true spirit of nation.

---

###  **Major Revolutions**

####  **1830 Revolution**

* Greece became independent from Ottoman Empire.
* Belgium broke from the Netherlands.

####  **1848 Revolution**

* Middle class demanded **constitution, freedom, press rights**.
* Workers demanded better working conditions.

---

###  **Unification Movements**

#### **Italy**

* Mazzini (idealist), Garibaldi (fighter), Cavour (diplomat)
* 1861  Victor Emmanuel II became King.

#### **Germany**

* Otto von Bismarck led unification under Prussia.
* Used **war** with Denmark, Austria, France.
* German Empire proclaimed in 1871 at **Versailles**.

---

###  **Conservative Nationalism**

* After 1848, monarchs used nationalism to strengthen state power.
* Example: Germany, Italy unified under kings, not democratically.

# **2. Nationalism in India**

---

###  **Causes of Nationalism**

* Rowlatt Act
* Jallianwala Bagh massacre
* Racial discrimination
* Economic exploitation
* WWI hardships (inflation, taxes)

---

###  **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920)**

**Aims:**

* Protest against Rowlatt Act, Khilafat injustice, Jallianwala Bagh.

**Methods:**

* Boycott British goods, titles, schools, courts.
* Promote **swadeshi** and **khadi**.

**Why withdrawn?**

* **Chauri Chaura violence (1922).**

---

###  **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930)**

Started with **Dandi March**.

**Features:**

* Breaking **salt law**
* Boycotting foreign cloth
* Refusing taxes
* Defying forest laws
* Participation of **women**, **business class**, **rich peasants**, **poor peasants**, **industrial workers**

**Difference from NCM:**

* CDM involved *law-breaking*
* Imported broader participation
* Focused on *Purna Swaraj*
* Salt law became national symbol

---

###  **Limits of Nationalism**

* Dalits felt Congress did little for them.
* Muslims feared Hindu majority domination (Muslim League emerged).
* Plantation workers had different understanding of Swaraj.

# **3. The Making of a Global World (Till 1.3)**

---

###  **Pre-modern Globalisation**

* **Silk Routes**  linked Asia, Europe, Africa.
* Spread goods (silk, spices), ideas (Buddhism), culture.

---

###  **19th Century Economy**

* Cheap food imports due to **Corn Laws repeal**.
* Migration of Europeans to USA, Australia.
* Rise of **indentured labour** from India to plantations.

---

###  **Impact on Colonies**

* Colonies became **sources of raw materials** & **markets** for industrial goods.
* Indian textiles suffered due to cheap Manchester cloth.

# **4. Print Culture and the Modern World**

---

###  **Impact of Print**

1. **Spread of literacy**
2. **Spread of new ideas**  reformation, enlightenment.
3. **Growth of public opinion**
4. **Rise of nationalism**
5. **Rise in debates and discussions**

---

###  **Print in China**

* Oldest tradition  **woodblock printing**.
* 19th century: Western powers brought **mechanical presses**.
* Beijing became center of modern print.

---

###  **Print in India**

* Missionaries started first presses.
* Growth of religious reform movements:

  * Raja Rammohan Roy
  * Arya Samaj
  * Muslim reformers
* Print empowered **lower castes**.
* Vernacular newspapers exposed British abuses.
